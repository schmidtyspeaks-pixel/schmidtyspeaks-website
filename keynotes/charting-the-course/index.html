Charting the Course to Our Future

A story-led keynote about calibrating your business and your life toward the best version of yourself, built on family roots, sailing with his father, and lessons with “Little G.”

Book Schmidty Watch

A keynote about direction, not just motivation

Most motivational talks give an audience a spike of energy that fades by the next morning. Charting the Course to Our Future is built to last longer than that. Geoff “Schmidty” Schmidt uses the image of a sailboat at sea to talk about something every team and every person deals with: how to keep adjusting toward where you want to go when the wind keeps changing.

The keynote draws on the stories Schmidty knows best. Sailing with his father. Raising his two kids, “The Gs,” and the lessons he has learned from “Little G.” And 22 years at Rocket Mortgage, where he moved from Director of Banking to Regional Vice President of Sales and back to the front line as a Senior Director of Banking and Triple Crown Mortgage Banker. Those chapters gave him a real-world view of leadership, setbacks, and the habits that help people finish strong.

What the audience hears

The talk moves between story and framework, with humor and honesty along the way. It is organized around four ideas that audiences tend to repeat long after the event:

  • “Smooth seas never made a skilled sailor.” Hard seasons are where skill gets built. Schmidty talks openly about adversity and what it taught him, so people can see their own rough water differently.
  • The three types of reflection: past, present, and future. A simple way to look back with honesty, take stock of where you are today, and decide where you are heading next.
  • “The next shot is your best shot.” A mindset for moving on from a bad call, a lost deal, or a rough quarter without carrying it into the next opportunity.
  • “How you finish is who you are.” A challenge to close the year, the project, or the career chapter with the same intent you brought to the start.

The three types of reflection

Reflection is the practical core of the keynote. Looking at the past means owning what happened, the wins and the misses, and pulling the lesson out of it. Looking at the present means being honest about where you are right now, what you control, and what is pulling you off course. Looking at the future means choosing a heading and committing to the next few moves that will get you there.

Schmidty walks the room through each one with a story and a question. Organizers can pair the keynote with the free 3 Types of Reflection workbook so attendees keep working on their answers after the session, or book it alongside an Elevate to Action workshop for a deeper experience.

Who it is for

Charting the Course works well as an opening or closing keynote for leadership summits and sales kickoffs, association conferences, and company meetings. It also translates to campuses, where the same ideas help students think about the path they are on. Schmidty has shared this message with banking teams at Rocket Mortgage, with sales students at The University of Akron and Ohio University, and with live audiences at Elevate in Cleveland.

Customizing the message

Before every event, Schmidty talks with the organizer about the audience, the moment the group is in, and the one thing people should walk away with. A team coming off a hard year may need more of the “smooth seas” chapter. A group heading into a big launch may need more of the future. The keynote runs 30 to 60 minutes and can be paired with a workshop, a panel, or a Q&A.
